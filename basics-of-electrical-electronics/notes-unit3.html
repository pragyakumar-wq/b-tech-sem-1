3.1 Electromagnetic Principles

Faraday's law states that changing magnetic flux linking a circuit induces emf.

e=−N(dΦ/dt)

Lenz's law gives the opposing direction. A current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field experiences force:

F=BIl sinθ

Self inductance

e=−L(di/dt)

Change of current in a coil induces emf in the same coil. Mutual inductance occurs when changing current in one coil induces emf in another.

e₂=−M(di₁/dt)

3.2 DC Machines

A DC generator converts mechanical to electrical energy; a DC motor converts electrical to mechanical energy.

Construction

  • Yoke: mechanical support and magnetic path.
  • Poles/pole shoes: establish and spread flux.
  • Field winding: produces magnetic field.
  • Armature core/winding: energy-conversion region.
  • Commutator: switching/rectification arrangement.
  • Brushes: electrical connection to rotating commutator.

Generator EMF

Eg=PΦZN/(60A)

P=poles, Φ=flux/pole, Z=conductors, N=speed rpm, A=parallel paths.

Motor

Motor action is based on force on a current-carrying conductor. Back emf opposes supply:

Eb=V−IaRa

3.3 Single-Phase Transformer

A transformer is a static AC device transferring energy between circuits at the same frequency by mutual induction. It is used for stepping voltage up/down and isolation.

Construction

It consists mainly of a laminated magnetic core, primary winding, secondary winding and insulation.

E1=4.44fN1Φm;   E2=4.44fN2Φm
V1/V2≈N1/N2≈I2/I1

N2>N1 gives step-up action; N2

3.4 Transformer Performance

Voltage ratio k=V2/V1≈N2/N1
Voltage regulation(%)=[(VNL−VFL)/VFL]×100

Important losses are core losses (hysteresis and eddy current) and copper I²R losses. Regulation indicates the change in secondary voltage from no-load to full-load condition at constant primary voltage.

3.5 Induction Motors

In an induction motor, rotor current is induced by the rotating magnetic field produced by the stator.

Ns=120f/P   ;   s=(Ns−N)/Ns

Ns is synchronous speed, N is rotor speed and s is slip. A three-phase stator produces a rotating magnetic field. Single-phase induction motors need a suitable starting arrangement because a basic single-phase motor is not inherently self-starting.

Applications include pumps, fans, compressors, conveyors and industrial drives.

3.6 Fractional-Horsepower Motors

These are small motors with output below one horsepower, used in fans, small pumps, appliances and light-duty mechanisms.

Exam focus: Faraday/Lenz laws, DC-machine construction and EMF equation, transformer EMF equation and regulation, induction-motor rotating field, synchronous speed and slip.
