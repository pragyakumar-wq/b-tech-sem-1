3.1 Electromagnetic Principles, Self-Inductance & Mutual Inductance (TSO 3a)

  • Faraday's First Law: Whenever the magnetic flux linking a closed conductor or coil changes, an electromotive force (EMF) is induced in it.
  • Faraday's Second Law & Lenz's Law: The magnitude of induced EMF is directly proportional to the rate of change of flux linkage, and its direction (by Lenz's Law) opposes the cause producing it:
    e = − N (dΦ / dt)    Volts.
  • Dynamically Induced EMF (Generator Rule): When a conductor of active length l moves with velocity v across a magnetic field B: e = B l v sin θ (Direction given by Fleming's Right-Hand Rule).
  • Force on a Current-Carrying Conductor (Motor Rule): F = B I l sin θ Newtons (Direction given by Fleming's Left-Hand Rule).

3.2 DC Machines: Construction, Working, EMF Equation & Classification (TSO 3b)

Main Constructional Parts of a DC Machine:

  • 1. Yoke (Frame): Outer cylindrical frame made of cast steel; provides mechanical protection and a low-reluctance return path for magnetic flux.
  • 2. Field Poles (Pole Core & Pole Shoes): Laminated steel cores carrying the field windings; pole shoes spread flux uniformly in the air gap.
  • 3. Armature Core & Winding: Rotating cylindrical drum built of silicon steel laminations (to minimize eddy current and hysteresis losses) with slots holding copper armature conductors connected in Lap Winding (A = P, high current, low voltage) or Wave Winding (A = 2, high voltage, low current).
  • 4. Split-Ring Commutator: Cylindrical assembly of hard-drawn copper segments insulated by mica; acts as a mechanical rectifier (converts AC induced in armature to DC in a generator, and DC supply to alternating torque current in a motor).
  • 5. Carbon Brushes: Rest on the rotating commutator to collect/supply current smoothly without sparking.

Derivation of EMF Equation of a DC Machine

Let P = number of poles, Φ = flux per pole (Wb), Z = total armature conductors, N = speed in RPM, and A = number of parallel paths (A = P for Lap, A = 2 for Wave):

  1. Flux cut by 1 conductor in 1 revolution = P Φ Webers.
  2. Time taken for 1 revolution = 60 / N seconds.
  3. Average EMF induced per conductor = (Flux cut) / (Time) = P Φ N / 60 Volts.
  4. Since there are Z / A conductors in series per parallel path, the total generated EMF is:
    Eg = (P Φ Z N) / (60 A)    Volts

Terminal Voltage Equations & Back EMF:

  • DC Generator: V = Eg − Ia Ra (where Ia is armature current and Ra is armature resistance).
  • DC Motor: When rotating in the magnetic field, the motor armature induces an opposing Back EMF (Eb = PΦZN / 60A):
    V = Eb + Ia Ra   ⇒   Ia = (V − Eb) / Ra, and electromagnetic torque Ta = 0.159 P Φ Z Ia / A ∝ Φ Ia.

3.3 & 3.4 Single-Phase Transformers: Construction, EMF Equation & Performance (TSO 3c)

Working Principle: A transformer is a static AC electromagnetic device that transfers electrical power from one AC circuit to another at the same frequency with a change in voltage and current levels, operating on the principle of Faraday's Law of Mutual Induction.

Construction Types: Uses high-grade CRGO silicon steel laminations (0.35 mm – 0.5 mm thick) to minimize hysteresis and eddy current losses:
• Core-Type Transformer: Windings surround a considerable part of the rectangular core (2 limbs); used for high-voltage, medium/small power.
• Shell-Type Transformer: Core surrounds a major portion of the windings on the central limb (3 limbs); provides dual magnetic paths and strong mechanical bracing.

Derivation of EMF Equation & Transformation Ratios

Let alternating flux in the core be Φ(t) = Φm sin(2πft). In one quarter-cycle (time dt = T/4 = 1/(4f)), flux changes from 0 to +Φm:

  1. Average EMF induced per turn = dΦ / dt = Φm / (1/4f) = 4 f Φm Volts.
  2. For a sinusoidal wave, Form Factor = RMS / Average = 1.11. Hence, RMS EMF induced per turn = 1.11 × 4 f Φm = 4.44 f Φm Volts.
  3. For Primary (N1 turns) and Secondary (N2 turns) windings:
    E1 = 4.44 f N1 Φm   Volts ,    and    E2 = 4.44 f N2 Φm   Volts
  4. Voltage, Turns & Current Transformation Ratio (K):
    E2 / E1 ≈ V2 / V1 = N2 / N1 = I1 / I2 = K
    (If N2 > N1 ⇒ K > 1: Step-Up Transformer; if N2 < N1 ⇒ K < 1: Step-Down Transformer).
  5. Voltage Regulation & Efficiency:
    • % Voltage Regulation = [ (V2(No-Load) − V2(Full-Load)) / V2(Full-Load) ] × 100% (Lower regulation = better voltage stability!).
    • Efficiency η = Output Power / (Output Power + Core Loss Pi + Copper Loss Pcu). Maximum efficiency occurs when Variable Copper Loss = Constant Iron/Core Loss (I22 R02 = Pi).

3.5 & 3.6 Single-Phase, Three-Phase Induction Motors & FHP Motors (TSO 3d)

1. Three-Phase Induction Motor (Self-Starting):

  • When a 3-phase stator winding is fed from a balanced 3-phase AC supply, it produces a Rotating Magnetic Field (RMF) of constant magnitude 1.5 Φm rotating at Synchronous Speed:
    Ns = 120 f / P    (RPM).
  • The RMF cuts the stationary rotor conductors (Squirrel-Cage or Slip-Ring/Wound Rotor), inducing rotor EMF and current. By Lenz's law, the rotor rotates in the same direction as the RMF at rotor speed N < Ns.
  • Fractional Slip (s): s = (Ns − N) / Ns   ⇒   N = Ns(1 − s), and rotor current frequency is fr = s · f.

2. Single-Phase Induction Motor (Why Not Self-Starting & How Made Self-Starting):

  • A single-phase stator winding produces a pulsating (alternating) magnetic field, which by the Double-Field Revolving Theory resolves into two equal magnetic fields rotating in opposite directions at ±Ns, producing zero net starting torque.
  • To make it self-starting, an auxiliary (starting) winding displaced by 90° in space (often with a series capacitor to create a ~90° phase split) is used: e.g., Split-Phase, Capacitor-Start, Capacitor-Run (Ceiling Fans), and Shaded-Pole Motors.

3. Fractional Horsepower (FHP) Motors: Small electric motors rated below 1 HP (746 W) (such as Universal motors, Shaded-pole motors, Capacitor motors, Stepper & Servo motors) used in ceiling fans, mixers, washing machines, refrigerators, printers, hair dryers, and medical instruments.
