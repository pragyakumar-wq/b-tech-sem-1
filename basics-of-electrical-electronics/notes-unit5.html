    • 1's Complement: Invert all bits (0 → 1, 1 → 0).
    • 2's Complement: 1's Complement + 1. To compute A − B, add A + (2's complement of B) and discard any end-around carry.

Fundamental Laws of Boolean Algebra & De Morgan's Theorems:

  • Identity & Null Laws: A + 0 = A,  A · 1 = A,  A + 1 = 1,  A · 0 = 0
  • Idempotent & Complement Laws: A + A = A,  A · A = A,  A + A' = 1,  A · A' = 0,  (A')' = A
  • Absorption Laws: A + AB = A,  A(A + B) = A,  A + A'B = A + B
  • De Morgan's First Theorem: The complement of a sum equals the product of the complements: (A + B)' = A' · B'
  • De Morgan's Second Theorem: The complement of a product equals the sum of the complements: (A · B)' = A' + B'

5.3 LEDs, Seven-Segment Displays & Analog / Digital ICs (TSO 5c)

  • Light Emitting Diode (LED): A heavily doped forward-biased p-n junction diode fabricated from direct band-gap compound semiconductors (such as GaAs, GaP, GaAsP, InGaN) that emits visible or infrared light by electroluminescence (radiative recombination of electrons and holes: E = hc/λ = Eg). Requires a series current-limiting resistor (typically 10–20 mA, forward drop 1.8 V–3.3 V).
  • Seven-Segment Display: Consists of 7 rectangular LED segments labeled (a, b, c, d, e, f, g) arranged in a figure-8 pattern to display decimal digits 0–9:
    • Common Cathode (CC): All 7 LED cathodes are tied to Ground (0 V); a segment glows when its anode is driven HIGH (Logic 1).
    • Common Anode (CA): All 7 LED anodes are tied to +VCC; a segment glows when its cathode is driven LOW (Logic 0).

5.4 Voltage Regulator ICs (IC 7805 & IC 7912), Multimeter & CRO (TSO 5d, 5e)

IC 7805 and IC 7912 Three-Terminal Regulators

  • 78XX Series (Positive Voltage Regulators): IC 7805 provides a fixed, regulated +5 V DC output (up to 1 A–1.5 A) from an unregulated DC input of +7.5 V to +35 V.
    Pinout: Pin 1 = Unregulated Input, Pin 2 = Ground, Pin 3 = Regulated +5 V Output.
  • 79XX Series (Negative Voltage Regulators): IC 7912 provides a fixed, regulated −12 V DC output from an unregulated negative DC input of −14.5 V to −35 V.
    Pinout: Pin 1 = Ground, Pin 2 = Unregulated Negative Input, Pin 3 = Regulated −12 V Output.
  • Both include built-in thermal overload protection and short-circuit current limiting.

Digital Multimeter (DMM) & Cathode Ray Oscilloscope (CRO)

  • Multimeter (AVO Meter): Measures DC/AC Voltage (connected in parallel, high internal resistance), DC/AC Current (connected in series, very low internal resistance), Resistance, Diode drop, and Continuity.
  • Cathode Ray Oscilloscope (CRO): High-speed X-Y electronic graphing instrument using a Cathode Ray Tube (Electron Gun, Vertical/Horizontal Deflection Plates, Fluorescent Screen) and Time-Base Generator to display real-time V(t) waveforms:
    • Peak-to-Peak Voltage: Vp-p = (Vertical divisions) × (Volts/div)
    • Time Period & Frequency: T = (Horizontal divisions per cycle) × (Time/div), and f = 1 / T.

5.5 Sensors, Transducers & Electrical Power System Layout (TSO 5f)

  • Sensor vs. Transducer: A Sensor detects and responds to a physical stimulus (heat, light, pressure), whereas a Transducer converts one form of energy (non-electrical physical quantity) into another form (electrical voltage/current signal):
    • Temperature: RTD (Pt100 — positive temp coeff.), Thermistor (NTC), Thermocouple (Seebeck effect — active transducer).
    • Displacement / Force: LVDT (inductive), Strain Gauge (piezoresistive), Piezoelectric crystal (active).
    • Light: LDR (Photoresistor), Photodiode, Photovoltaic Solar Cell.
  • Layout of Electrical Power System:
    1. Generation (11 kV, 50 Hz 3-Phase AC) → 2. Primary Step-Up Transmission (132 kV / 220 kV / 400 kV / 765 kV) (stepping up voltage reduces current I = P / (√3 V cos φ) and minimizes I2R transmission losses) → 3. Secondary Transmission (66 kV / 33 kV) → 4. Primary Distribution (11 kV) → 5. Secondary Distribution (Pole/Plinth Step-Down Transformer: 400 V 3-Phase 4-Wire / 230 V 1-Phase to consumers).

Earthing (Grounding) Systems — Purpose, Importance & Methods (TSO 5h):

  • Definition: Connecting the non-current-carrying metallic frames/enclosures of electrical equipment or the neutral point of a supply system directly to the general mass of the Earth (0 V reference potential) through a very low-resistance conductor (earth resistance < 1 Ω for power stations, < 5 Ω for domestic installations).
  • Purpose & Importance of Earthing:
    1. Protects human life from electric shock: If live insulation fails and touches a metal appliance body, fault current flows immediately into the low-resistance earth path instead of through a person touching the appliance.
    2. Blows the Fuse / Trips the MCB/RCCB immediately: Large earth fault current trips the protective device and isolates the faulty circuit.
    3. Maintains stable line-to-ground voltage and protects buildings/installations against lightning surges.
  • Standard Methods of Earthing:
    • 1. Plate Earthing: A copper plate (60 cm × 60 cm × 3.18 mm) or GI plate (60 cm × 60 cm × 6.35 mm) buried vertically at least 1.5 m–3 m deep in alternating layers of charcoal and salt (to retain moisture and keep soil resistivity low).
    • 2. Pipe Earthing (Most Common in India): A galvanized perforated iron (GI) pipe (38 mm diameter, 2.5 m long) buried vertically in a pit filled with alternating layers of charcoal and salt, with a funnel at the top for pouring water during summer.
