Assignment-1 · Question 3 (Sub-BEEE · Submission: 21/10/2026)

Explain the difference between intrinsic and extrinsic semiconductor with suitable energy band diagrams.

1. Overview of Intrinsic vs. Extrinsic Semiconductors

  • Intrinsic Semiconductor: An extremely pure semiconductor crystal (such as pure Silicon Si or Germanium Ge) containing no impurity atoms. At room temperature, thermal energy breaks a few covalent bonds, generating equal numbers of free electrons and holes (n = p = ni).
  • Extrinsic Semiconductor: An impure (doped) semiconductor obtained by intentionally adding a small, controlled amount (typically 1 part in 106 to 108) of a suitable pentavalent or trivalent impurity to a pure semiconductor to drastically increase its electrical conductivity.

3. Detailed Comparison Between Intrinsic and Extrinsic Semiconductors

S. No.ParameterIntrinsic SemiconductorExtrinsic Semiconductor
1Purity / FormPurest form of tetravalent semiconductor crystal without any added impurities.Doped semiconductor formed by adding trivalent (Group III) or pentavalent (Group V) impurities.
2TypesOnly one pure form for a given element/compound.Two types: N-type (pentavalent donor doped) and P-type (trivalent acceptor doped).
3Carrier ConcentrationNumber of free electrons equals number of holes: n = p = ni.Unequal carrier concentrations: in N-type n ≫ p; in P-type p ≫ n (while obeying np = ni2).
4Cause of Charge CarriersSolely due to thermal agitation breaking covalent bonds.Primarily due to impurity doping plus a small contribution from thermal agitation.
5Position of Fermi Level (EF)Lies exactly at the center of the forbidden energy gap: EF = (EC + EV)/2.Shifts near EC (above donor level ED) in N-type, and near EV (below acceptor level EA) in P-type.
6Additional Energy LevelNo impurity energy level exists inside the forbidden band gap.Introduces a discrete Donor level (ED) just below EC or Acceptor level (EA) just above EV.
7Electrical Conductivity (σ)Very low at room temperature: σi = ni e (μn + μp).High and controllable by doping concentration: σn ≈ ND e μn, σp ≈ NA e μp.
8Dependence on TemperatureConductivity depends solely and exponentially on temperature.Conductivity depends mainly on doping concentration and also on temperature.
9ExamplesPure crystalline Silicon (Si), pure Germanium (Ge).Si/Ge doped with P, As, Sb (N-type) or B, Ga, In (P-type).
10Practical ApplicationsLimited direct use (used as base substrate or in specialized photodetectors/thermistors).Used to fabricate almost all electronic devices (diodes, Zener diodes, BJTs, FETs, LEDs, solar cells, ICs).
