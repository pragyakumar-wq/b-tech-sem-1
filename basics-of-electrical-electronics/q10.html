Assignment-1 · Question 10 (Sub-BEEE · Submission: 21/10/2026)

With a neat diagram, explain the working, construction and operation principles of PNP and NPN transistors.

1. Construction of Bipolar Junction Transistors (NPN and PNP)

A Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT) is a three-layer, two-junction semiconductor device in which current conduction takes place due to both majority and minority charge carriers (electrons and holes). Depending on the arrangement of layers, BJTs are of two types: (1) NPN Transistor (a thin p-type layer sandwiched between two n-type layers) and (2) PNP Transistor (a thin n-type layer sandwiched between two p-type layers).

Three Regions of a BJT (Doping & Width Profile):

  • 1. Emitter (E): Heavily doped and of moderate width/area. Its function is to inject (emit) a large number of majority charge carriers into the Base.
  • 2. Base (B): Middle layer, made extremely thin (≈ 1–2 μm) and very lightly doped so that more than 95%–98% of carriers injected from the Emitter pass straight into the Collector without recombining.
  • 3. Collector (C): Moderately doped and physically has the largest cross-sectional area because it collects almost all charge carriers and must dissipate the maximum heat power at the reverse-biased Collector-Base junction.

3. Working & Operation Principles in Active Mode

1. Working of NPN Transistor

  • Biasing: The Emitter-Base (E-B) junction is Forward Biased by battery VEE (negative terminal to n-type Emitter, positive to p-type Base), and the Collector-Base (C-B) junction is Reverse Biased by battery VCC (positive terminal to n-type Collector).
  • Carrier Injection & Collection: Because the n-type Emitter is heavily doped, it injects a large number of free electrons across the forward-biased E-B junction into the Base, producing Emitter current IE.
  • Since the p-type Base is very thin and lightly doped, less than 2% to 5% of electrons recombine with holes in the Base (forming a tiny Base current IB).
  • The remaining 95% to 98% of electrons diffuse to the C-B depletion layer and are swept into the Collector by the positive potential of VCC, forming Collector current IC.

2. Working of PNP Transistor

  • Biasing: The Emitter-Base (E-B) junction is Forward Biased by battery VEE (positive terminal to p-type Emitter, negative to n-type Base), and the Collector-Base (C-B) junction is Reverse Biased by battery VCC (negative terminal to p-type Collector).
  • Carrier Injection & Collection: The heavily doped p-type Emitter injects a large number of holes into the thin n-type Base, producing Emitter current IE.
  • Only 2% to 5% of injected holes recombine with electrons in the lightly doped Base (forming small Base current IB).
  • Over 95% to 98% of the holes are swept across the reverse-biased C-B junction into the Collector by the negative potential of VCC, forming Collector current IC.

4. Fundamental BJT Equations & Operating Regions

Fundamental Current Law: By Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL) applied to the transistor as a single node:

IE = IB + IC ,    α = IC / IE (0.95 – 0.99) ,    β = IC / IB = α / (1 − α) (50 – 300)
