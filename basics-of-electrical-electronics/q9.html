Assignment-1 · Question 9 (Sub-BEEE · Submission: 21/10/2026)

With a neat diagram, explain the working of a p-n junction diode, draw its V-I characteristics, and explain the operation of forward bias and reverse bias.

1. Formation of a p-n Junction & Depletion Region (Unbiased)

When a p-type semiconductor (having high concentration of holes) is monolithically joined with an n-type semiconductor (having high concentration of free electrons), a p-n junction is formed:

  • Due to concentration gradient, holes diffuse from the p-side to the n-side and electrons diffuse from the n-side to the p-side across the junction and recombine.
  • This leaves behind uncovered, immobile negative acceptor ions (−) on the p-side and immobile positive donor ions (+) on the n-side near the junction.
  • This region depleted of mobile charge carriers is called the Depletion Region (or Space-Charge Region) of width W ≈ 0.5 μm.
  • The uncovered ions set up an internal built-in electric field (from n to p) and a Barrier Potential VB (0.7 V for Silicon and 0.3 V for Germanium at 300 K) that prevents further diffusion of majority carriers at equilibrium.

3. Operation Under Forward Bias and Reverse Bias

1. Forward Bias Operation

  • Connection: Positive (+) terminal of the external DC battery is connected to the p-region and negative (−) terminal to the n-region.
  • Effect on Depletion Layer & Barrier: Holes in the p-side and electrons in the n-side are repelled toward the junction. This narrows the depletion layer and reduces the potential barrier to (VB − VF).
  • Current Flow: Once VF exceeds the Cut-in / Knee Voltage Vγ (0.7 V for Si, 0.3 V for Ge), majority carriers cross the junction easily and forward current IF rises exponentially (in mA), offering very low forward resistance (10 Ω – 50 Ω).

2. Reverse Bias Operation

  • Connection: Negative (−) terminal of the external DC battery is connected to the p-region and positive (+) terminal to the n-region.
  • Effect on Depletion Layer & Barrier: Holes in the p-side and electrons in the n-side are pulled away from the junction toward the battery terminals. This widens the depletion layer and increases the potential barrier to (VB + VR).
  • Current Flow: Majority carrier flow becomes zero. Only a tiny Reverse Saturation Current (IS or I0) (in μA for Ge and nA for Si) flows due to thermally generated minority carriers until the reverse voltage reaches Breakdown Voltage (VBR).

Shockley's Ideal Diode Equation: Across both forward and reverse bias regions, the diode current is governed by:

I = IS [ eVD / (η VT) − 1 ]

where IS is reverse saturation current, VD is applied voltage (+ for forward, − for reverse), η is the ideality factor (1 for Ge, 2 for Si), and VT = kT/q ≈ 26 mV at 300 K.
