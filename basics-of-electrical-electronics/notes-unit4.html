4.1 Semiconductor Materials

Semiconductors have conductivity between conductors and insulators. Silicon is widely used.

Intrinsic semiconductor is pure; electron and hole concentrations are equal:

n=p=ni

Extrinsic semiconductor is intentionally doped.

n-typeDonor/pentavalentElectrons

4.2 p-n Junction Diode

Joining p and n regions creates a depletion region and built-in electric field.

Forward bias

p-side positive and n-side negative reduces the junction barrier and allows significant current after the practical threshold region.

Reverse bias

p-side negative and n-side positive widens the depletion region; only small reverse current flows until breakdown.

I=Is[e^(VD/(ηVT))−1]

4.3 Temperature Effects and Breakdown

Temperature changes carrier concentration and diode characteristics. Breakdown can occur by Zener or avalanche mechanisms.

  • Zener: strong electric field in a heavily doped junction.
  • Avalanche: carrier multiplication due to impact ionization.

4.4 Rectifiers, Filters and Zener Regulator

Half-wave rectifier

One diode conducts during one half-cycle, producing pulsating DC.

VDC=Vm/π; ηmax≈40.6%

Full-wave rectifier

Both half-cycles contribute to output using centre-tapped or bridge arrangements.

VDC=2Vm/π; ηmax≈81.2%

Filter

A capacitor filter reduces ripple by storing charge and supplying the load between peaks.

Zener regulator

A reverse-biased Zener operated in its breakdown region can maintain approximately constant output voltage with a series current-limiting resistor.

Vout≈VZ

4.5 BJT Fundamentals

BJT is a three-terminal device: emitter, base and collector. Common types are NPN and PNP.

IE=IC+IB; α=IC/IE; β=IC/IB

4.6 CE Configuration

In common-emitter configuration, emitter is common to input/output. It provides useful current and voltage gain.

As an amplifier, a small base-current variation controls a larger collector-current variation. As a switch, cut-off represents OFF and saturation represents ON.

Approx. β=ΔIC/ΔIB
