4.1 Intrinsic & Extrinsic Semiconductor Materials (TSO 4a)

Semiconductors are tetravalent crystalline materials (Group IV: Silicon Si, Eg = 1.12 eV; Germanium Ge, Eg = 0.72 eV) having electrical conductivity between conductors and insulators, and a negative temperature coefficient of resistance.

  • Intrinsic Semiconductor: Pure semiconductor crystal with no doping. Electron concentration equals hole concentration: n = p = ni, and the Fermi level EF lies at the middle of the forbidden energy gap.
  • N-Type Extrinsic Semiconductor: Doped with pentavalent (Group V) donor impurities (P, As, Sb). Each donor atom provides one free electron (donor level ED lies ~0.05 eV below EC). Majority carriers = Electrons (n ≫ p); Minority carriers = Holes.
  • P-Type Extrinsic Semiconductor: Doped with trivalent (Group III) acceptor impurities (B, Ga, In, Al). Each acceptor atom creates a hole (acceptor level EA lies ~0.05 eV above EV). Majority carriers = Holes (p ≫ n); Minority carriers = Electrons.
  • Mass-Action Law: n · p = ni2 holds at thermal equilibrium for both intrinsic and extrinsic semiconductors.

4.2 & 4.3 p-n Junction Diode, Temperature Effect & Breakdown Mechanisms (TSO 4b)

  • Unbiased p-n Junction: Diffusion of holes and electrons across the junction creates a charge-free Depletion Layer containing immobile negative acceptor ions on the p-side and positive donor ions on the n-side, setting up a Barrier Potential VB (0.7 V for Si, 0.3 V for Ge).
  • Forward Bias (+ to P, − to N): Narrows the depletion layer, reduces barrier potential to (VB − VF), and allows large exponential majority-carrier current (in mA) once VF ≥ Vγ.
  • Reverse Bias (− to P, + to N): Widens the depletion layer, increases barrier to (VB + VR), and blocks majority current; only a tiny minority-carrier Reverse Saturation Current (IS) flows.
  • Effect of Temperature on Diode Characteristics:
    1. The cut-in / barrier voltage Vγ decreases by ~2.5 mV/°C as temperature increases.
    2. The reverse saturation current IS doubles for every 10°C rise in temperature: IS2 = IS1 × 2(T2 − T1)/10.
  • Breakdown Mechanisms:
    • Zener Breakdown (< 6 V): Heavily doped narrow depletion layer; intense electric field (~107 V/m) causes direct covalent bond rupture / quantum tunneling; negative temperature coefficient.
    • Avalanche Breakdown (> 6 V): Lightly doped wider depletion layer; accelerated minority carriers cause impact ionization and carrier multiplication; positive temperature coefficient.

  • Rectifier Filters (Smoothing Circuits): Since rectifier output is pulsating DC (contains AC ripple + DC), a Filter Circuit is connected between the rectifier and load:

4.5 & 4.6 BJT Fundamentals, CE Amplifier & Transistor as a Switch (TSO 4d)

1. BJT in CE Configuration as an Amplifier

  • Operates in the Active Region (Emitter-Base junction Forward Biased, Collector-Base junction Reverse Biased) with Q-point biased near the center of the DC load line.
  • A small alternating base current signal ib produces a large amplified collector current ic = β ib through load resistor RC.
  • Output voltage is vce = VCC − IC RC, producing high voltage gain Av = −β (RC / Ri) with a 180° phase shift between input and output AC waveforms.

2. BJT as an Electronic Switch

  • Operates alternately between the Cut-off Region and the Saturation Region:
  • OFF State (Open Switch — Cut-off): When input base voltage Vin < 0.7 V, base current IB = 0 ⇒ IC ≈ 0, and output voltage Vout = VCE ≈ VCC (Logic HIGH = 1).
  • ON State (Closed Switch — Saturation): When input base voltage drives large IB ≥ IC(sat)/β, both junctions become forward biased, IC(sat) ≈ VCC/RC, and Vout = VCE(sat) ≈ 0.2 V (Logic LOW = 0).
