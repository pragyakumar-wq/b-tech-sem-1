Assignment-1 · Question 1 (Sub-BEEE · Submission: 21/10/2026)

With a neat diagram, explain the working of a Zener diode as a voltage regulator and draw its V-I characteristics.

1. What is a Zener Diode?

A Zener Diode (also called a breakdown diode) is a heavily doped silicon p-n junction diode specially designed to operate continuously in the reverse breakdown region without getting damaged, as long as the power dissipation stays within its safe limit (PZ(max) = VZ IZ(max)).

When reverse biased beyond its sharp breakdown voltage (called the Zener Voltage VZ), the voltage across the Zener diode remains almost constant at VZ even when the reverse current IZ varies widely between IZ(min) (knee current) and IZ(max).

2. Circuit Diagram: Zener Diode as a Shunt Voltage Regulator

3. Working Principle of Zener Diode as a Voltage Regulator

The Zener diode is connected in reverse bias in parallel (shunt) with the load resistor RL, and a current-limiting resistor RS is connected in series with the unregulated DC input voltage Vin (where Vin > VZ). The fundamental circuit equations are:

IS = (Vin − VZ) / RS ,    IS = IZ + IL ,    Vout = VZ = IL RL

Case 1: Line Regulation (Varying Vin, Constant RL)

  • Suppose the load resistance RL is kept fixed while the unregulated input voltage Vin increases.
  • Then the total series current IS = (Vin − VZ) / RS increases.
  • Since the Zener is in breakdown, the extra current flows entirely through the Zener diode (IZ increases), while the load current IL = VZ / RL remains unchanged.
  • The extra voltage drop is absorbed across series resistor RS, keeping output voltage Vout = VZ constant.
  • Conversely, if Vin decreases, IS and IZ decrease equally so that IL and Vout = VZ remain constant.

Case 2: Load Regulation (Constant Vin, Varying RL)

  • Suppose the input voltage Vin is fixed, so the total source current IS = (Vin − VZ) / RS is constant.
  • If the load resistance RL decreases, the load current IL = VZ / RL increases.
  • To keep IS = IZ + IL constant, the Zener current IZ decreases by the exact same amount (as long as IZ ≥ IZ(min)).
  • Similarly, if RL increases, IL decreases and IZ increases by the same amount.
  • Thus the voltage drop across RS stays constant and Vout = VZ remains regulated.

4. V-I Characteristics of a Zener Diode

Explanation of V-I Curve:
• Forward Bias (First Quadrant): Behaves exactly like an ordinary silicon p-n junction diode, conducting strongly above the knee voltage Vγ ≈ 0.7 V.
• Reverse Bias (Third Quadrant): Initially a tiny reverse leakage current flows. At VR = VZ, sharp breakdown occurs and the curve becomes almost vertical — keep IZ between IZ(min) and IZ(max) for voltage regulation.
