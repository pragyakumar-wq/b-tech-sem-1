2.3 Human Being as Co-existence of Sentient 'Self' ('I') and Material 'Body' (TSO 2c)

A human being is not merely a physical body, nor merely a spirit — a human being is the co-existence of the sentient 'Self' (Jeevan / 'I') and the material 'Body' (Sharir). There is a continuous exchange of information (signals/instructions and sensations) between the Self ('I') and the Body.

Comprehensive Comparison Between the Sentient 'Self' ('I') and the Material 'Body'

ParameterThe Sentient 'Self' ('I' / Jeevan)The Material 'Body' (Sharir)
1. Fundamental NatureConscious / Sentient (Chaitanya) unit.Material / Physico-chemical (Jada) unit.
2. Basic NeedsHappiness, Trust, Respect, Knowledge, Right Feelings (Suvidha nahi, Samadhan aur Samman).Physical Facilities (Suvidha) — Food, Water, Clothing, Shelter, Instruments.
3. Needs in TimeContinuous in time (We want respect and happiness 24×7, never want a break from trust).Temporary / Intermittent in time (We need food or sleep only periodically, not continuously).
4. Needs in QuantityQualitative (Not Quantitative) — You cannot measure trust or respect in kilograms or liters!Quantitative and Limited — Food, clothes, and shelter are needed in definite, measurable quantities.
5. Fulfilment of NeedsFulfilled ONLY by Right Understanding and Right Feelings (cannot be bought by money/food).Fulfilled by Physico-chemical things (cannot be fed by good feelings alone).
6. Type of ActivitiesDesire (Ichchha), Thought (Vichar), Expectation (Asha); Knowing, Assuming, Recognizing, and Fulfilling.Breathing, heartbeat, digestion, movement — purely Recognizing and Fulfilling.
7. Continuity of ActivityActivities in 'I' are continuous (with scope for conscious choice).Activities in the Body are cyclic / temporary and mechanical/involuntary.
8. Response MechanismBased on Knowing (Gyana), Assuming (Manyata), Recognizing (Pahchanana), and Fulfilling (Nirvahan).Based strictly on Recognizing and Fulfilling (Pahchanana aur Nirvahan) (e.g., body reacts to poison or glucose mechanically).

Understanding the Self ('I') as Seer (Drashta), Doer (Karta), and Enjoyer (Bhokta)

  • 1. Seer (Drashta): It is the Self ('I') that understands and sees reality directly (or reads sensations via the eyes/ears as instruments). The eyes do not understand — 'I' sees through the eyes.
  • 2. Doer (Karta): It is the Self ('I') that takes decisions and chooses what action to perform, instructing the Body when needed.
  • 3. Enjoyer (Bhokta): It is the Self ('I') that experiences happiness or unhappiness as a result of its thoughts, feelings, and actions.

Activities in the Self ('I'): Knowing, Assuming, Recognizing, and Fulfilling

  • Problem Today (Living on Preconditioning / Assuming without Knowing):
    When we do not have Right Understanding (Knowing), our Assumptions (Manyata) come from outside preconditioning or sensory temptations. Since our Recognition and Fulfilment depend on our Assumptions (e.g., assuming a needle is a doctor's cure vs. an enemy's weapon changes our entire reaction!), wrong assumptions cause conflict and unhappiness.
  • Solution (Assuming Based on Knowing):
    Through Self-Exploration and Right Understanding (Knowing), our assumptions align with reality, making our recognition and fulfilment definite, harmonious, and mutually fulfilling.

Harmony of the Self ('I') with the Body: Sanyam and Health (Swasthya)

Sanyam (Self-Regulation)

Definition: Sanyam is the feeling of responsibility in the Self ('I') to ensure the Nurturing (Poshan), Protection (Sanrakshan), and Right Utilization (Sahi Upayog) of the Body.

  1. Nurturing of Body: Proper air, water, wholesome food (Ahar), daily routine (Dincharya), and exercise/postures (Asana-Pranayama).
  2. Protection of Body: Proper clothing, shelter, hygiene, and medicine/treatment only when needed.
  3. Right Utilization of Body: Using the body as an instrument for right behavior and productive work, not for sensual over-indulgence or violence.

Swasthya (Health)

Definition: The Body is in a state of Health (Swasthya) when:

  1. The Body acts according to the instructions of the Self ('I') (Swa mein sthit hona).
  2. All parts/organs of the Body are in harmony and functioning properly together.

Key Relation: Sanyam in the Self ('I') is the foundation for Swasthya (Health) in the Body. Without Sanyam, no amount of hospital infrastructure or medicine alone can guarantee lasting health.
