Question 2 · Unit 2 (CO2)

Explain the human being as a co-existence of the sentient 'Self' ('I') and the material 'Body'. Compare their needs, activities, and response mechanisms in detail, and explain Sanyam and Health (Swasthya).

Part A: Human Being as Co-existence of Sentient 'Self' ('I') and Material 'Body' (TSO 2c)

A human being is the co-existence of two distinct realities: the conscious/sentient unit called the Self ('I' / Jeevan) and the physico-chemical unit called the Body (Sharir). The Self ('I') is the Seer (Drashta), Doer (Karta), and Enjoyer (Bhokta), while the Body acts as a physical instrument that exchanges information (instructions and sensations) with the Self.

AspectSentient 'Self' ('I' / Jeevan)Material 'Body' (Sharir)
1. Basic NeedsRight Understanding & Right Feelings (Trust, Respect, Happiness).Physical Facilities (Food, Clothing, Shelter, Instruments).
2. Needs in TimeContinuous (needed at every moment).Temporary / Periodic (needed in intervals).
3. Needs in QuantityQualitative (cannot be measured in numbers/weight).Quantitative & Limited (measurable in kg, liters, meters).
4. Fulfilled ByRight Understanding & Right Feelings.Physico-Chemical Things.
5. ActivitiesDesire (Ichchha), Thought (Vichar), Expectation (Asha) (Continuous).Breathing, Heartbeat, Digestion (Cyclic / Temporary).
6. Response TypeKnowing, Assuming, Recognizing, and Fulfilling.Only Recognizing and Fulfilling (definite & mechanical).

Part B: Sanyam (Self-Regulation) and Health (Swasthya)

  • Sanyam (in the Self 'I'): The feeling of responsibility in the Self ('I') towards the Body for its Nurturing (Poshan), Protection (Sanrakshan), and Right Utilization (Sahi Upayog).
  • Swasthya / Health (in the Body): The condition where (1) the Body acts according to the instructions of the Self ('I'), and (2) all organs of the Body function in mutual harmony.
  • Program for Sanyam & Swasthya: Wholesome intake & balanced daily routine (Ahar-Vihar), physical labor & exercise (Shram & Vyayam), postures & regulated breathing (Asana-Pranayama), and treatment/medicine (Aushadhi-Upchar) only when the body falls ill.
