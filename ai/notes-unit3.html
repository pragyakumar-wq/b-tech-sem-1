Unit 3 · 8 hrs

Knowledge Representation and Reasoning

Syllabus topics:

  • Knowledge representation issues, propositional logic
  • Resolution in propositional logic
  • First-order predicate logic
  • Forward chaining, backward chaining, resolution (concept)
  • Semantic networks and frames
  • Rule-based (production) systems

Propositional logic

A proposition is a statement that is true or false. Connectives: ¬ (not), ∧ (and), ∨ (or), → (implies), ↔ (if and only if).

Semantics: truth tables. P → Q is false only when P is true and Q is false. P → Q is equivalent to ¬P ∨ Q.

  • Example: "If it rains, the road is wet" is R → W.
  • A sentence is valid (tautology), satisfiable, or unsatisfiable.

Resolution (proof by refutation)

  • Convert all sentences to CNF (remove ↔ and →, push ¬ inward, distribute ∨ over ∧).
  • Add the negation of the goal to the knowledge base.
  • Resolve pairs of clauses with complementary literals: (A ∨ B) and (¬B ∨ C) give (A ∨ C).
  • If the empty clause is produced, the goal is proved.

First-order predicate logic

  • Elements: constants, variables, functions, predicates, connectives and quantifiers.
  • ∀x (for all) and ∃x (there exists).
  • Example: "All students are smart" = ∀x (Student(x) → Smart(x)). "Some student is smart" = ∃x (Student(x) ∧ Smart(x)).
  • Common mistake: using ∧ with ∀ or → with ∃.

Forward and backward chaining

PointForward chainingBackward chaining
DirectionFrom known facts towards the goalFrom the goal back to the facts
TypeData-drivenGoal-driven
Works onDefinite clauses (Horn clauses)Definite clauses (Horn clauses)
Good forMonitoring, deriving all conclusionsAnswering a specific query, diagnosis

Resolution in first-order logic also needs unification to match variables with constants.

Semantic networks and frames

  • Semantic network: a graph with nodes for objects or concepts and labelled edges for relations such as is-a and has-part. Supports inheritance.
  • Frame: a record with slots (attributes) and values, default values and procedures. Frames are linked in a hierarchy, so child frames inherit slots.

Rule-based (production) systems

Parts: rule base (IF-THEN rules), working memory (current facts) and an inference engine (match rules, pick one by conflict resolution, fire it, repeat).

Design steps: choose the domain, collect knowledge, write rules, choose forward or backward chaining, test.
