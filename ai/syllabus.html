Unit 1: Introduction to Artificial Intelligence (8 hrs)

Mapped CO: CO1

Theory Session Outcomes (TSOs):

  • TSO 1a. Explain the evolution and foundational philosophies of AI.
  • TSO 1b. Describe the structure and functioning of intelligent agents using the PEAS framework.
  • TSO 1c. Classify task environments based on their properties.
  • TSO 1d. Differentiate between types of agents based on their internal architecture.
  • TSO 1e. Outline AI application domains across engineering disciplines.

Topics (Body of Knowledge):

  • 1.1 What is AI? History and evolution of AI
  • 1.2 Foundations of AI: philosophy, mathematics, and cognitive-science influences
  • 1.3 Intelligent agents: agent-environment interaction, PEAS (Performance measure, Environment, Actuators, and Sensors) description
  • 1.4 Types of environments (observable, deterministic, episodic, static, discrete, single/multi-agent)
  • 1.5 Types of agents: simple reflex, model-based, goal-based, utility-based, learning agents
  • 1.6 Overview of AI application domains across engineering disciplines

Unit 2: Problem Solving by Search (9 hrs)

Mapped CO: CO2

Theory Session Outcomes (TSOs):

  • TSO 2a. Represent a given real-world problem as a state-space search problem.
  • TSO 2b. Trace uninformed search strategies manually and compare their complexity.
  • TSO 2c. Trace informed search strategies using suitable heuristic functions.
  • TSO 2d. Verify the admissibility and consistency of a given heuristic.
  • TSO 2e. Illustrate local search techniques for simple optimisation problems.
  • TSO 2f. Trace adversarial search with alpha-beta pruning for a two-player game.
  • TSO 2g. Illustrate simple Constraint Satisfaction Problems (CSPs).

Topics (Body of Knowledge):

  • 2.1 Problem formulation and state-space representation
  • 2.2 Uninformed search: BFS, DFS, Uniform Cost Search, Depth-Limited and Iterative Deepening Search
  • 2.3 Informed (heuristic) search: Greedy Best-First Search, A* algorithm
  • 2.4 Heuristic function design and admissibility/consistency
  • 2.5 Local search algorithms: Hill Climbing, Simulated Annealing (concept)
  • 2.6 Adversarial search: Minimax algorithm, Alpha-Beta pruning
  • 2.7 Constraint Satisfaction Problems (CSP): concept, backtracking (overview)

Unit 3: Knowledge Representation and Reasoning (8 hrs)

Mapped CO: CO3

Theory Session Outcomes (TSOs):

  • TSO 3a. Represent simple real-world facts using propositional and predicate logic.
  • TSO 3b. Apply resolution manually to perform logical inference.
  • TSO 3c. Trace forward and backward chaining to derive conclusions from a knowledge base.
  • TSO 3d. Represent structured knowledge using semantic networks and frames.
  • TSO 3e. Outline the design of a simple rule-based (production) system for a given domain.

Topics (Body of Knowledge):

  • 3.1 Knowledge representation issues; propositional logic: syntax and semantics
  • 3.2 Inference in propositional logic: resolution
  • 3.3 First-order predicate logic: syntax, quantifiers (concept)
  • 3.4 Inference in first-order logic: forward chaining, backward chaining, resolution (concept)
  • 3.5 Structured knowledge representation: semantic networks, frames
  • 3.6 Rule-based (production) systems

Unit 4: Reasoning Under Uncertainty (7 hrs)

Mapped CO: CO4

Theory Session Outcomes (TSOs):

  • TSO 4a. Explain the need for probabilistic reasoning in AI.
  • TSO 4b. Apply Bayes' theorem to compute conditional probabilities in AI problems.
  • TSO 4c. Construct a simple Bayesian network for a given scenario.
  • TSO 4d. Explain the basic principles of fuzzy logic and fuzzy-set operations.

Topics (Body of Knowledge):

  • 4.1 Sources of uncertainty; review of basic probability theory
  • 4.2 Bayes' theorem and its application to AI reasoning
  • 4.3 Bayesian networks: representation and manual inference (overview)
  • 4.4 Introduction to fuzzy logic and fuzzy sets
  • 4.5 Certainty factors and rule-based uncertainty (overview)

Unit 5: Introduction to Machine Learning and AI Applications (10 hrs)

Mapped CO: CO5

Theory Session Outcomes (TSOs):

  • TSO 5a. Differentiate between supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning paradigms.
  • TSO 5b. Describe the working principle of basic regression, classification, and clustering techniques.
  • TSO 5c. Describe the basic structure of a perceptron and an artificial neural network.
  • TSO 5d. Describe applications of AI in natural language processing, computer vision, and expert systems.
  • TSO 5e. Identify ethical, bias-related, and societal implications of AI deployment.
  • TSO 5f. Relate AI applications to sustainable development goals and multidisciplinary engineering contexts.

Topics (Body of Knowledge):

  • 5.1 Machine learning paradigms: supervised, unsupervised, reinforcement learning (concept)
  • 5.2 Overview of regression and classification concepts (linear regression, k-NN, decision trees)
  • 5.3 Overview of clustering concept (k-means)
  • 5.4 Introduction to artificial neural networks: perceptron, basic architecture (concept)
  • 5.5 Overview of Natural Language Processing (NLP): concept and applications
  • 5.6 Overview of Computer Vision: concept and applications
  • 5.7 Expert systems: architecture and applications
  • 5.8 Robotics and AI: overview of intelligent robotic systems
  • 5.9 Ethics, bias, and responsible AI; societal impact; AI and Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)
