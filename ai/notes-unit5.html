Introduction to Machine Learning and AI Applications

Syllabus topics:

  • Artificial neural networks: perceptron
  • NLP and Computer Vision

Regression and classification

  • Regression predicts a continuous value. Classification predicts a category.
  • Linear regression: fits y = mx + c (or y = w0 + w1x) by minimising squared error.
  • k-NN: classify a point by the majority class among its k nearest neighbours (distance such as Euclidean). No training phase.
  • Decision tree: internal nodes test attributes, leaves give the class. Attribute chosen by information gain (reduction in entropy).

Clustering: k-means

  • Choose k and pick k initial centroids.
  • Assign every point to the nearest centroid.
  • Recompute each centroid as the mean of its points.
  • Repeat the last two steps until the assignments stop changing.

Neural networks: perceptron

A perceptron computes a weighted sum of inputs plus a bias and applies a step function: output = 1 if Σ wixi + b > 0, else 0.

An artificial neural network has an input layer, one or more hidden layers and an output layer; neurons are connected by weights that are learned from data. A single perceptron can only solve linearly separable problems (AND, OR, not XOR).

NLP and computer vision

  • NLP: lets machines understand and generate human language. Applications: translation, chatbots, sentiment analysis, speech assistants.
  • Computer vision: lets machines understand images and video. Applications: face recognition, object detection, medical imaging, self-driving cars.

Expert systems and robotics

  • Expert system architecture: knowledge base, inference engine, user interface, explanation facility, knowledge acquisition module. Applications: medical diagnosis (MYCIN), fault finding, financial advice.
  • Robotics and AI: robots use sensors (perception), planning and control (decision) and actuators (action). Used in industry, healthcare, agriculture and service tasks.

Ethics, bias and SDGs

  • Bias: biased training data produces unfair decisions (hiring, loans, policing).
  • Responsible AI: fairness, transparency, accountability, privacy, safety and human oversight.
  • Societal impact: job changes, misinformation, privacy and surveillance concerns.
  • SDGs: AI supports health, quality education, clean energy, sustainable agriculture and climate action, if used responsibly.
